# Automation Interns Sales Navigator playbook

Use this reference as the default operating hypothesis when the user has not supplied a more specific campaign brief. Live evidence and explicit user instructions take precedence.

## Offer context

Automation Interns supplies locally managed, senior-supported automation and AI implementation capacity. Historical B2B positioning includes $30/hour with no commitment, regular demonstrations against a defined work plan, replacement within 72 hours, and an optional $5,000 direct-hire path. Verify the current offer before using any of these claims in outbound messaging.

## Default ideal customer profile

Prioritize Arizona organizations with roughly 11–200 employees where a small leadership team owns meaningful manual workflows and can act quickly. Strong initial segments include professional services, software-enabled services, health and wellness operations, logistics, field services, multi-location businesses, and other operations-heavy SMBs.

Deprioritize or exclude:

- staffing, recruiting, lead-generation, or automation agencies unless the campaign explicitly targets partners;
- students, internship seekers, individual contributors without workflow influence, and profiles seeking employment;
- companies whose location, size, or operating model clearly falls outside the campaign;
- leads with no confirmed current role or no credible connection to the account;
- direct competitors and existing clients when discoverable.

## Buyer map

Use the narrowest relevant buyer hypothesis:

- **Economic buyer:** founder, owner, partner, president, CEO, COO, or relevant VP at a small organization.
- **Operations owner:** operations, business operations, service delivery, customer operations, or process-improvement leader.
- **Revenue systems owner:** revenue operations, sales operations, marketing operations, growth operations, or go-to-market systems leader.
- **Technology owner:** CIO, CTO, head/VP/director of IT, engineering, applications, data, or digital transformation.
- **Workflow owner:** senior manager or director accountable for a specific manual process, program, or cross-functional system.

Seniority is contextual. A manager at a 25-person company may have more buying influence than a vice president at a large enterprise.

## Signal hierarchy

Prefer observable and recent signals:

1. a new role or leadership change tied to the target function;
2. active hiring or visible organizational growth;
3. a recent post about scaling, systems, AI, automation, operations, data, or customer delivery;
4. a company announcement that implies process change, expansion, integration work, or capacity constraints;
5. multiple relevant stakeholders appearing at the same well-fit account.

Do not treat a signal as proof of pain or buying intent. It supports a timely, curious outreach angle.

## Scoring rubric

Score only from verified evidence. Use `unknown` rather than awarding inferred points.

| Dimension | Points | What earns credit |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Account fit | 0–30 | geography, size, segment, and operating model fit |
| Role and influence | 0–25 | direct ownership, economic authority, or credible evaluation role |
| Automation relevance | 0–20 | visible workflow complexity or transformation responsibility |
| Timing signal | 0–15 | recent, sourceable change, activity, hiring, or expansion |
| Personalization evidence | 0–10 | specific professional context usable without overclaiming |

Suggested bands:

- **80–100:** outreach-ready after a final duplicate and relationship check;
- **65–79:** useful secondary stakeholder or nurture candidate;
- **below 65:** do not add to the active queue without a campaign-specific reason.

A hard exclusion overrides the numeric score.

## Ongoing cadence

For a normal prospecting session:

1. review saved-search alerts and the strong-signal watchlist;
2. qualify a small set of accounts;
3. map the buyer group at the best accounts;
4. score and deduplicate candidates;
5. hand off only the top leads for outreach drafting or approval;
6. record which hypothesis produced the leads so later sessions can compare quality.

Rotate searches only when the active queue is healthy or evidence shows that a hypothesis is weak. Avoid repeatedly broadening filters merely to increase result counts.
